# Recommended Books

> **Essential reading for product managers and product-led teams.**

---

## Foundation Books

### Inspired: How to Create Tech Products Customers Love
**Author**: Marty Cagan  
**Level**: Beginner to Intermediate  
**Anchor**: `#inspired`

**Why Read It:**
- The modern product management bible
- Covers discovery, prioritization, and delivery
- Strong focus on empowered product teams

**Best For:**
- Understanding the full product lifecycle
- Learning how great product teams operate
- Convincing stakeholders about product-led approaches

**When to Read:**
- **Phase 1**: Before starting discovery
- **Anytime**: As a reference for team culture

**Key Takeaways:**
- Product teams solve problems, not implement features
- Discovery is continuous, not a phase
- Engineers are partners in discovery, not order-takers

---

### Continuous Discovery Habits
**Author**: Teresa Torres  
**Level**: Intermediate  
**Anchor**: `#continuous-discovery`

**Why Read It:**
- Practical framework for ongoing product discovery
- Interview techniques and opportunity mapping
- Connecting user research to product decisions

**Best For:**
- Running user interviews
- Mapping opportunities to solutions
- Building discovery habits into weekly routines

**When to Read:**
- **Phase 1**: When setting up discovery practices
- **Phase 4**: When validating assumptions with users

**Key Takeaways:**
- Weekly user interviews are non-negotiable
- Opportunity solution trees help prioritize
- Assumptions must be tested, not defended

## Prioritization & Strategy

### Escaping the Build Trap
**Author**: Melissa Perri  
**Level**: Intermediate  
**Anchor**: `#escaping-build-trap`

**Why Read It:**
- How to move from output-focused to outcome-focused teams
- Product strategy and vision frameworks
- Organizational alignment for product-led growth

**Best For:**
- Defining product strategy
- Aligning leadership around outcomes
- Breaking the "feature factory" cycle

**When to Read:**
- **Phase 2**: When prioritizing and roadmapping
- **Phase 3**: When defining success metrics

**Key Takeaways:**
- Outputs (features) are not outcomes (value)
- Strategy connects vision to daily decisions
- Product managers are not project managers

---

### Impact Mapping
**Author**: Gojko Adzic  
**Level**: Intermediate  
**Anchor**: `#impact-mapping`

**Why Read It:**
- Visual approach to connecting goals to deliverables
- Helps avoid scope creep and misalignment
- Useful for stakeholder communication

**Best For:**
- Stakeholder workshops
- Scoping initiatives
- Communicating why certain features are excluded

**When to Read:**
- **Phase 1**: During discovery scoping
- **Phase 2**: When mapping goals to stories

**Key Takeaways:**
- Start with the goal, not the feature
- Map actors, impacts, and deliverables
- Use it to say "no" with clarity

## Specification & Delivery

### User Story Mapping
**Author**: Jeff Patton  
**Level**: Beginner to Intermediate  
**Anchor**: `#user-story-mapping`

**Why Read It:**
- How to organize stories into a coherent narrative
- Discovering gaps and dependencies visually
- Building shared understanding across teams

**Best For:**
- Structuring user stories
- Finding missing scenarios
- Aligning product, design, and engineering

**When to Read:**
- **Phase 1**: When collecting user stories
- **Phase 3**: When refining specifications

**Key Takeaways:**
- Stories are a narrative, not a backlog
- Mapping reveals what is missing
- The backbone is the user journey

---

### Writing Effective Use Cases
**Author**: Alistair Cockburn  
**Level**: Intermediate  
**Anchor**: `#writing-use-cases`

**Why Read It:**
- Deep dive into goal-level requirements
- Handling exceptions and extensions
- Connecting use cases to user stories

**Best For:**
- Writing acceptance criteria
- Handling complex edge cases
- Structured requirements documentation

**When to Read:**
- **Phase 3**: When writing detailed specs
- **Phase 4**: When validating completeness

**Key Takeaways:**
- Use cases describe goals, not interfaces
- Extensions handle errors and alternatives
- Preconditions matter

---

## Communication & Collaboration

### The Mom Test
**Author**: Rob Fitzpatrick  
**Level**: Beginner  
**Anchor**: `#mom-test`

**Why Read It:**
- How to talk to users without biasing their answers
- Lightweight customer interview techniques
- Separating opinions from facts

**Best For:**
- Running user interviews
- Validating problem statements
- Avoiding leading questions

**When to Read:**
- **Phase 1**: Before any stakeholder interviews
- **Phase 4**: When validating specs with users

**Key Takeaways:**
- Never ask "Would you use this?"
- Talk about their life, not your idea
- Ask for specifics, not opinions

---

### Crucial Conversations
**Authors**: Kerry Patterson, Joseph Grenny, Ron McMillan, Al Switzler  
**Level**: Beginner to Intermediate  
**Anchor**: `#crucial-conversations`

**Why Read It:**
- Handling high-stakes discussions with stakeholders
- Saying no while preserving relationships
- Aligning conflicting priorities

**Best For:**
- Stakeholder management
- Priority negotiations
- Scope conversations

**When to Read:**
- **Phase 2**: When prioritization gets political
- **Phase 4**: When securing sign-off

**Key Takeaways:**
- Safety is the prerequisite for dialogue
- Focus on what you really want
- Separate facts from stories

## Reading Roadmap

### For New Product Managers
```
1. The Mom Test (interviewing)
2. Inspired (product culture)
3. User Story Mapping (backlog structure)
4. Escaping the Build Trap (strategy)
```

### For Experienced Product Managers
```
1. Continuous Discovery Habits (discovery process)
2. Impact Mapping (strategic alignment)
3. Writing Effective Use Cases (specification depth)
4. Crucial Conversations (stakeholder mastery)
```

### For Product Leaders
```
1. Escaping the Build Trap (organizational change)
2. Inspired (team empowerment)
3. Continuous Discovery Habits (team habits)
4. Crucial Conversations (executive communication)
```
